Older adults and Holocaust survivors

Care, benefits, and financial assistance, including compensation claims. Most help starts with a local Jewish agency.

Start with a Jewish agency near you

Network of Jewish Human Service Agencies — Member Locator

  • Older adults

Local Jewish Family Service agencies help older adults and Holocaust survivors with things like care at home, transportation, food, medication, and day-to-day needs. Many also help survivors apply for compensation.

A local agency is usually the first step for The Blue Card, because The Blue Card only accepts survivors referred by a social service or government agency.

Holocaust survivors

The Blue Card

Financial assistance for Holocaust survivors

  • Free
  • Older adults
  • Referral needed

The only organization in the United States whose sole mission is giving financial help to Holocaust survivors in need. It offers both long-term support and emergency grants for basic needs.

Please note: Survivors cannot apply directly. A social service or government agency in your area must refer you, and a case manager there completes a financial assessment. The Blue Card is an agency of last resort: it helps once other local and government help has been explored.

Holocaust survivors · Compensation

Claims Conference

Conference on Jewish Material Claims Against Germany

  • No fee to apply
  • Older adults

Runs compensation programs for Holocaust survivors and funds the social service agencies that provide home care and other help.

The Claims Conference does not charge survivors or families a fee to apply, and you do not need a lawyer or any paid go-between. Many people get help with the forms from a local Jewish Family Service.
